\subsection{Research gap}
% Discuss > Frame as -> Existing studies establish electrification feasibility under observed mobility or develop behaviourally richer simulation frameworks, but there is limited evidence on how progressively richer representations of agency propagate from simulated system behaviour into optimised infrastructure decisions, and whether simpler-model solutions remain robust when evaluated under richer behavioural assumptions.

Existing work can evaluate whether observed paratransit mobility is compatible with electrification~\cite{}, while behaviourally explicit models can represent adaptive responses to energy and charging constraints~\cite{}. What remains unclear is whether these different representations lead to materially different infrastructure planning decisions when applied to the same empirically observed transport subsystem, and whether infrastructure selected under fixed-mobility assumptions remains effective once driver and passenger interactions are allowed to respond endogenously.

\subsection{Research questions}

This study addresses the following research questions:
\begin{enumerate}
    % RQ1 - System representation
    \item How do predicted electrification outcomes differ when the mobility of the same sampled paratransit fleet is represented as fixed empirical activity versus endogenous driver behaviour?
    % RQ2 - Planning decision
    \item How do the electrification infrastructure strategies selected by data replay and agent-based optimisation differ when each model is evaluated using the outcomes it can validly represent?
    % RQ3 - Decision consequence (uni-directional regret)
    \item What additional operational and passenger-service consequences become visible when a replay-derived infrastructure strategy is evaluated in the agent-based model?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
This study examines the implications of explicit behavioural representations of driver agency for paratransit electrification planning. It contributes to the literature by performing a controlled comparison of fixed-mobility data-replay and decision-adaptive agent-based modelling approaches applied to the same empirically observed paratransit subsystem. The model-comparison framework adheres to model-native objective spaces, rather than forcing a single objective space reduction across models with different behavioural assumptions. Additionally, the study performs independent simulation-based optimisation of charging infrastructure under each model, and evaluates the consequences of applying a data-replay-derived infrastructure strategy in the agent-based model. The findings provide insights into how progressively richer representations of driver agency can influence electrification outcomes and infrastructure planning decisions, guiding future research and policy development in the context of paratransit electrification.

\begin{figure}[htb!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/system-diagram.pdf}
    \caption{
        Overview of the study design. The same empirical paratransit mobility data is used to parameterise both a data-replay model and an agent-based model. Each model is then used to independently optimise charging infrastructure, and the resulting infrastructure strategies are evaluated under each model's native assumptions. The agent-based model is also used to evaluate the consequences of applying a data-replay-derived infrastructure strategy in a decision-adaptive context. The result is then compared to the agent-based model's native infrastructure strategy to assess the implications of ignoring driver agency in electrification planning.
