| 14 | §6.3 What the models imply for emissions | New subsection | — | \subsection{What the models imply for emissions} \label{sec:discussion_emissions}<br><br>The choice of model also changes the estimated emissions benefit of a pilot. The replay model writes off a stranded vehicle-day as a day that stays diesel, so even with zero-carbon electricity it credits the pilot with only 22\% to 71\% of the electrified vehicles' diesel CO$_2$. In the agent-based model the drivers do not strand, and the same pilot avoids almost all of it (Figure~\ref{fig:emissions_grid}). A replay-based assessment therefore understates what cleaner electricity could deliver, for the same reason that it overstates the energy infeasibility of electrification.<br><br>On South Africa's current grid, however, the electricity source matters more than where the chargers go. At the 2023 grid factor, an electric kilometre emits 9\% more CO$_2$ than a diesel kilometre, consistent with the per-kilometre comparison of \citet{sello2026_embt_in_cpt}, so neither model finds a clear saving. In the agent-based model, moving to zero-carbon electricity changes the avoided CO$_2$ by 84 to 120 percentage points, against at most 11 points for the choice of design. The design that keeps the most passengers on the EVs is also not the one that emits least on this grid. In Khayelitsha and Wynberg, the EVs of \xA{} queue less and drive more than those of \xR, and above the break-even grid factor each additional electric kilometre adds CO$_2$. Under \xR, the EVs drive less, and the passengers they lose ride in seats of the ICE competitors that would otherwise be empty, so the lower CO$_2$ of \xR{} costs the EV operators revenue but not the service to passengers. With zero-carbon electricity this trade-off disappears in Khayelitsha and Wynberg, and in Bellville \xA{} avoids 6 points more.<br><br>Two implications follow. First, the CO$_2$ case for electrifying minibus taxis in South Africa rests on cleaner charging, from a decarbonising grid or a renewable supply, more than on the charging design; solar generation at the ranks alone is limited by the area that it needs~\cite{abraham2021_solar_embt_charging}. Second, the passengers that the EVs lose are not an emissions penalty: the ICE competitors carry them without driving further, so the emissions of a mixed-fleet pilot follow the distance and energy of the EVs alone. Because the EVs are a small share of the taxis, a pilot changes the CO$_2$ of its community by less than 1\% on the current grid and by at most 6.6\% with zero-carbon electricity, so the emissions benefit depends on electrification beyond the pilot. | The agreed framing: the model changes the estimated emissions benefit, and on South Africa's grid the electricity source matters more than where the chargers go. |
